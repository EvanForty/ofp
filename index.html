From swap request to buyback

Six steps, in the order an agent comes online.

  1. Launch a coin and its agent

    Create a token paired with its own order flow agent.

  2. Choose a market

    Specialize in memecoins, SOL pairs, stablecoins or high-volume markets.

  3. Aggregate order flow

    Wallets, trading bots and apps integrate to send it their swap requests.

  4. Optimize execution

    Orders go to competing DEXs, AMMs and RFQ market makers for the best fill.

  5. Generate revenue

    Disclosed routing fees, execution service fees or negotiated order flow payments.

  6. Buy back the coin

    A portion of realized net protocol revenue purchases the associated token.

Order flow marketplace

Anyone can launch a tokenized order flow business. A community could launch its coin with an agent that specializes in Solana memecoin trades. Trading apps plug into its API and route their users' swaps through its engine.

Before every route, the agent compares available liquidity, price, slippage and execution cost. Apps and market makers pay for eligible routing services, and part of net revenue buys back the community's coin. Each agent's dashboard shows:

Trading volumeOrders routedExecution qualityRevenueBuybacks

The key idea: OFP agents don't own liquidity. They compete to control how trading demand reaches the providers that do.

Execution principles

Order flow payments can create conflicts of interest. Every OFP agent is built around four rules.

Users firstRoutes prioritize the user's execution quality.
Disclosed payAny compensation for routing is shown openly.
MEV protectionOrders are shielded from sandwich attacks and exploitation.
No promisesRevenue and buybacks are not guaranteed.
